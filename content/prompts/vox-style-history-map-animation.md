A 10-second landscape historical documentary animation in a Vox-style narration aesthetic. The background is a post-war Tokyo map on warm golden-yellow parchment, complete with latitude/longitude grid lines, ruined city road routes, electronics-shop stamps, paper folds, and faded brown water stains.

A young technician carrying machine sketches sets off from a small-workshop paper-cut illustration and glides lightly along a green route through Tokyo’s streets. Each time he passes a location, he scatters electronic components into the air; they spin and transform into a rice cooker, a transistor radio, a Walkman, and the Sony logo icon.

The route moves past building rubble, parts shops, small factories, and an export harbor. Along the side of the frame, vintage paper-cut stickers appear in sequence: factory workers, American traders, a young person with headphones, and a globe. The character finally arrives in a modern showroom, turns on a rice cooker, and smiles with his colleagues.

In the closing moment, steam from the rice rises and forms a curved map line connecting the small workshop to the wider world. Dynamic flowing graphics, paper-collage style, with a palette of tea green, earthy yellow, deep brown, and cream white — warm, controlled, and rich with the feeling of history spreading.
