# KDP market research — validating the niche before you write

A great book in a muddled niche loses to a competent book in a clear niche. This file is the loop you run *before* committing to write.

---

## The 7-step research loop

1. **Open the exact Amazon subcategory** your book would live in. Not the top-level (Fiction > Mystery), but the third-level (Fiction > Mystery, Thriller & Suspense > Amateur Sleuths).
2. **Pull the top 20 books** in that subcategory. Open each in a new tab.
3. **Record surface commercial data** in a spreadsheet (template below).
4. **Read the first page of reviews** for each — especially the **3-star reviews**. They reveal what readers wanted but didn't get. That's your opportunity map.
5. **Click "Look Inside"** on the top 5. Your real competitors are the books with strong first pages and credible interiors. Books with great covers but weak opening pages are beatable.
6. **Map common promises**. In fiction: trope + subgenre + emotional payoff. In nonfiction: topic + audience + concrete outcome.
7. **Find the gap**. The gap is rarely "no competition". It's "a clear promise served incompletely" or "a sub-audience that's underserved by the top books".

## Competitive analysis spreadsheet template

For each of the top 20 books in your target shelf:

| Field | What to record | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| ASIN | Unique product ID | Lets you re-find and recompare |
| Title | Full title + subtitle | Naming patterns emerge across the shelf |
| Author | Real or pen name | Identify dominant authors vs newer entrants |
| Format mix | Kindle, paperback, hardcover, audio? | How serious the shelf is — more formats = more commitment expected |
| Kindle price | $X.XX | Reveals price bands readers accept |
| Paperback price | $X.XX | Same |
| Hardcover price | $X.XX | Same |
| Page count | Exact | Helps position scope and value |
| Rating | Average ★ | Quick quality signal |
| Review count | Total | Demand and durability proxy |
| Review velocity | New reviews / month (rough) | Active demand right now |
| Category path | Full path | Shows discovery environment |
| Release date | Month / year | New entrants vs evergreens |
| Series? | Book # / standalone | Series math matters a lot in fiction |
| Title promise | What outcome or emotion is sold | Avoid weak positioning |
| Subtitle structure | Audience + benefit + specificity | Useful in nonfiction especially |
| Cover signals | Fonts, color, symbols, imagery, trope markers | Visual genre expectations |
| Look Inside quality | First page, chapter structure, format | Conversion driver |
| 3-star complaints | Main themes in 1–3 star reviews | Your opportunity map |
| Ad presence | Sponsored Products visible on the page? | Signals commercial seriousness |
| Angle gap | What this book ignores or under-serves | Where you can differentiate |

If 20 feels like too many, do 10 — but never fewer than 10. Patterns only emerge with sample size.

## What to look for in 3-star reviews

3-star reviews are the most useful because they're written by readers who *wanted to like* the book but were let down by something specific. Look for repeated phrases:

- *"Felt rushed at the end"* → opportunity for stronger pacing in the resolution.
- *"Wanted more detail about X"* → underserved subtopic.
- *"The romance subplot felt forced"* → trope expectation mismatch.
- *"Repeats material from chapter 2 in chapter 8"* → editing failure you can avoid.
- *"Promised a system, gave anecdotes"* → promise/delivery mismatch.

When 4+ of the top 20 have similar 3-star themes, that's a market signal. Build your book to fix what those reviews are asking for.

## The 10-point niche scorecard

Before you commit, score the niche on these five factors (10 each):

| Factor | Question | Score / 10 |
|---|---|---|
| **Demand** | Are there multiple books with strong review velocity and active sales? | |
| **Fit** | Can you write this book credibly and faster than average? | |
| **Packaging clarity** | Is the promise easy to express in one title + one cover? | |
| **Competition shape** | Is the shelf active without being dominated by untouchable incumbents only? | |
| **Follow-on potential** | Could this become a series, companion workbook, or adjacent title? | |
| **Total** | / 50 | |

Decision rule:

- **35–50**: Strong niche. Proceed.
- **25–34**: Workable niche, but the lower-scored factors will limit results. Decide whether to address them or pivot.
- **Below 25**: Don't write this book. Find a better niche.

A mediocre book in a hot but muddled niche usually loses. A clearly packaged book in a healthy niche often wins faster.

## Genre-specific signals to read

### Fiction — what good niche signals look like

- **Multiple new entrants in the last 6 months** with 50+ reviews each (active demand, not just historical).
- **Top books have 4.2+ ratings with 300+ reviews** (durable quality bar exists and readers reward it).
- **Shelf has tropes/subgenres named in titles** — `enemies to lovers romance`, `cosy cat mystery` — readers know what they're searching for.
- **Mix of indie and traditionally published** — if it's all big-publisher, the shelf is hard to break into; if all indie, it's wide-open but you'll need to build trust.

### Nonfiction — what good niche signals look like

- **Books address a specific, named audience** (founders, parents of teens, marathon runners) — not just "everyone".
- **Outcomes are concrete and measurable** — *"finish your first novel in 90 days"* beats *"unlock your creative potential"*.
- **Top books are 200–300 pages**, not 50 (the shelf rewards depth) or 600 (the shelf is academic).
- **You can find at least 3 books that are "good but not great"** — that's the gap you fill.

---

## When the research says "don't write this"

Hard-won lessons. Walk away if:

- The top 10 are all written by one author and that author releases 4+ per year (you'll be drowned in their backlist).
- The shelf is dominated by 5+ trad-pub books with 5000+ reviews each (you can chart, but you won't outrank them).
- The top books have 4.0+ ratings *and* there are no consistent 3-star complaints (no opportunity gap).
- Books in this shelf are typically priced below $2.99 (you can't earn 70% royalty; volume math gets hard).
- The shelf has fewer than 10 books with 100+ reviews (insufficient demand to sustain a new entrant).

Better to spend a week pivoting than 6 months writing into a dead shelf.
