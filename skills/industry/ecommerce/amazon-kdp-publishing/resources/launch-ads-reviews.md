# Launch, ads, and reviews — without breaking Amazon's rules

The launch turns the product page from a passive listing into a sales asset. Ads turn it into a discoverable one. Reviews turn it into a trusted one.

---

## Launch sequence

| Window | What to do |
|---|---|
| **2 weeks before** | Finalise metadata. Recruit beta/ARC readers (10–20). Build Author Central page. Prep ad creative and a 30–50 keyword list. Plan a launch email/social post. |
| **Launch week** | Go live. Turn on Sponsored Products (auto + 1 manual keyword). Announce to your list/audience. Send ARC reminders for honest reviews. Monitor conversion daily. |
| **First month** | Optimise ads (move winning auto-search-terms to manual, add negatives). Test price only if conversion is poor. Refine description/keywords if traffic doesn't convert. Push for review velocity (15+ reviews is a meaningful conversion threshold). |
| **Ongoing** | Build backlist. Improve read-through if you have a series. Refresh ads quarterly. Gather more honest reviews steadily. |

---

## Author Central — set up first

Author Central is free real estate on Amazon. Set it up before you launch:

- Author photo (professional, high-resolution).
- Bio (200–400 words; warm, personal, but focused on what you write).
- Link your published books to your Author profile.
- Add an author blog feed if you have one (optional).
- Set up Author Following so readers can subscribe.

Each marketplace has its own Author Central. Set up at least amazon.com and amazon.co.uk.

## Amazon Ads — Sponsored Products

Sponsored Products are the workhorse for authors. CPC (pay-per-click), shows in search results and on competitor book pages.

### Starter campaign stack

| Campaign | Targeting | Budget | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SP Auto** | Amazon-chosen targets | $5–10/day | Discover what converts. Mine for winning search terms. |
| **SP Manual (keyword)** | Your phrases | $5–10/day | Bid harder on exact reader-intent terms. |
| **SP Manual (product targeting)** | Specific competitor ASINs | $3–5/day | Place your book on competitor pages. |
| *Sponsored Brands (later)* | Phrase/product | $5–10/day | Only once you have 3+ titles claimed under the same author. |

### Auto campaign settings

- **Match types**: Close + Loose + Substitutes + Complements (start with all four; turn off losers after 2 weeks).
- **Default bid**: $0.30–$0.50.
- **Budget**: $5–10/day.
- **Goal**: discovery, not profit. The job of auto is to *find* the winning search terms.

### Manual keyword campaign settings

- **Keywords**: 20–40 to start. Include:
  - Your title and subtitle words (broad and phrase match)
  - Genre/trope phrases (`cosy mystery`, `slow burn romance`)
  - Comp-author names in broad match (e.g., `Louise Penny` — note: bidding on author names is allowed; quoting in your own product page is not)
  - Reader-need phrases (`books like Big Little Lies`, `mysteries set in Maine`)
- **Match types**: Broad, Phrase, Exact (start with all three for each keyword).
- **Default bid**: $0.40–$0.80 (higher than auto because intent is sharper).
- **Negative keywords**: add as you find search terms that waste budget.

### Mining auto → manual

After 14 days of running auto, open the Search Term report:

1. Identify search terms with **clicks > 10 and orders > 1**.
2. Move those to your manual keyword campaign at exact match with a slightly higher bid.
3. Add the winning search terms as **negative phrases** to your auto campaign (so auto stops competing with your now-cheaper manual placement).
4. Identify search terms with **clicks > 10 and orders = 0**.
5. Add those as **negatives** in both campaigns (they waste budget).

Repeat every 2–4 weeks.

### Metrics to watch

| Metric | Formula | Healthy range |
|---|---|---|
| **CPC** (cost per click) | Spend ÷ clicks | $0.20–$0.80 for most fiction; varies by genre |
| **CTR** (click-through rate) | Clicks ÷ impressions | 0.2%+ for books |
| **CR** (conversion rate) | Orders ÷ clicks | 10%+ is strong, 5–10% is normal |
| **ACOS** | Ad spend ÷ ad revenue | <30% is profitable for paperback at $14.99+; <70% can still be worth it if KU page reads are strong |
| **ROAS** | Ad revenue ÷ ad spend | 1.4× breakeven is a reasonable target |

### Operating heuristic

- **Cheap clicks, no sales** → fix the product page (cover, description, pricing). Not an ads problem.
- **Expensive clicks, strong conversion** → fix targeting and bids. Cut the bleeding keywords; double down on winners.
- **No clicks at all** → either bid is too low or your cover is invisible at thumbnail size.

## Reviews — the hard compliance rules

Amazon enforces review policy aggressively. A single violation can result in losing all reviews, account suspension, or losing your KDP publishing rights. **Read this section twice.**

### What's banned

- **Incentivised reviews** — offering anything in exchange for a review (free book, refund, discount, bonus, prize, future content).
- **Asking for *positive* reviews specifically** — the ask must be for an *honest* review.
- **Reviews from family, friends, employees, or anyone with a personal/financial connection to you.**
- **Reviews exchanged** with other authors ("you review mine, I'll review yours").
- **Putting review requests in the KDP description field.** Explicitly prohibited.
- **Asking customers to remove or edit reviews.**
- **Using review-buying services** or paid review networks.
- **Multiple reviews from the same buyer** under different accounts.
- **Reviews that include URLs, contact info, or solicitations.**

### What's allowed

- Asking ARC readers for an **honest** review with no compensation. *"If you'd like to share your honest opinion, an Amazon review would mean a lot."*
- A review request in the **back matter of the book itself** (after the last chapter, before the about-the-author page): *"If you enjoyed this book, please consider leaving an honest review on Amazon. It helps other readers find it."*
- Asking your email list / social audience to consider reviewing — neutrally.
- Reviews from people who genuinely read and bought the book through normal channels.

### Safe ARC reader workflow

1. Recruit ARC readers (your list, genre-specific reader groups, bookstagram/booktok with a track record).
2. Send the ebook (EPUB or PDF) for free in exchange for **nothing** — explicitly say no obligation to review.
3. After they've had 2–3 weeks: send one polite reminder. *"If you had a chance to read, an honest review would be appreciated. If not, no worries."*
4. Never follow up if they leave a critical review. Never argue with a review.
5. Keep a private list of who you sent ARCs to — but never share that list publicly or pressure anyone individually.

### What to do about bad reviews

- **Read them honestly.** A pattern of 3-star reviews citing the same issue is the most useful data you'll get.
- **Don't reply to reviews on the product page.** Amazon explicitly prohibits authors from responding to reviewer comments on their own books.
- **Don't try to remove reviews** unless they're clearly TOS-violating (personal attacks, off-topic, spam). Those go to Amazon Community via the "Report abuse" link, not to KDP support.
- **Use 1–2 star themes as content for v2** of the book, or for the next book in the series.

## External marketing — only when the page converts

Outside traffic hitting a weak Amazon page wastes the click. Get the page converting first, then drive external traffic.

What "converting" means: of every 100 people who land on your Amazon page, 10+ buy. Below 5%, fix the page before sending more traffic.

Once it converts, the cleanest external channels:

| Channel | Why it works | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| **Your email list** | High intent, owned audience | Medium |
| **Genre-specific email newsletters** (BookBub, Robin Reads, etc.) | Targeted to active readers | Paid, $20–$500/run depending on tier |
| **BookBub Featured Deal** | Highest-impact email promo; competitive to get accepted | $500–$2000 per run |
| **Adjacent-author cross-promo** | Mutual swap with non-competing authors | Time |
| **Podcast appearances** (for nonfiction) | Long-form trust building | High |
| **Social media** | Slow build, brand-driven | Ongoing |
| **Goodreads giveaways** | Reach Goodreads's active reader base | $119+ |

The real goal isn't traffic. It's **qualified traffic that already matches the promise on the product page**.
